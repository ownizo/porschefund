Tesla vs Porsche: one built robots, the other built fake gears

15 November 2026 · 6 min read · by the Portuguese dad who still wants a Taycan

Every Portuguese man needs a rivalry. Benfica or Porto. Bica or galão. Grandma’s bacalhau or your mother-in-law’s bacalhau, a question you answer only with a lawyer present.

Mine is Tesla vs Porsche. Specifically the Tesla Model S against the Porsche Taycan: two electric saloons, two very different ideas of what a car should be, and one man in Lagos with a progress bar at 0% acting as referee.

I own neither. That is my qualification. I have no brand loyalty, only longing. I am the most neutral judge in Europe, in the same way a hungry man is a neutral judge of two sandwiches behind glass.

Seven rounds. No VAR. Let’s go.

Round 1: Commitment

Tesla built the Model S for 14 years. It started in 2012, it changed the world, it made petrol people nervous at dinner parties. Then, in May 2026, Tesla built the last one at its Fremont factory and turned that space over to Optimus, its humanoid robot, with a stated goal of up to a million robots a year.

So Tesla looked at its flagship saloon and saw a robot.

Porsche looked at the Taycan, an electric car with no gearbox, and gave it eight virtual gears and a noise on purpose. Porsche looked at an electric motor and saw a gearbox.

One company is so committed to the future it stopped making the car. The other is so committed to the past it taught an electric car to pretend. My household looked at both and saw a mortgage.

Round to Porsche. You cannot win a car rivalry by leaving to build robots. That is not a win, that is a career change.

Round 2: Speed

Both are ridiculous. Tesla says the Model S Plaid does 0 to 60 mph in about two seconds. Porsche says the Taycan Turbo GT with the Weissach package does 0 to 100 km/h in 2.2. One uses miles, the other uses kilometres, and both use numbers I will never personally verify because the A22 has a limit of 120 and a GNR officer with no sense of humour.

The fastest thing in my life is my three-year-old running towards an open door. Neither car can catch him. I have tried with my own legs and lost.

Round drawn. Both cars are faster than my ability to explain them to my bank.

Round 3: The Nürburgring

In May 2026, a Taycan Turbo GT with the factory Manthey kit went round the Nürburgring Nordschleife in 6:55.553 and set a new lap record for electric cars. That is Porsche going to the most terrifying racetrack on Earth and coming back with a trophy.

Tesla, in the same month, was dismantling the Model S production line. Hard to set a lap record from a factory floor full of robots, although I would not bet against the robots.

Round to Porsche.

Round 4: Charging (the plot twist)

In North America, the 2027 Taycan gets the Tesla-style charging port. Porsche’s pride and joy now plugs into the rival’s sockets.

This is like a Porto fan charging his phone in the Benfica stadium. Practical, yes. Emotionally complicated. I respect the maturity. I could never.

Round to Tesla. When your enemy needs your plug, you have won something, even if it is only a plug.

Round 5: Sound

The Tesla makes the sound of the future, which is almost nothing. A whisper. A fridge with ambition.

The Taycan with E-Shift plays a V8-inspired tone through its speakers while you pull the paddles. It is theatre. It is a very fast car doing an impression of an older car, like a son who grew up to be a doctor and still does his dad’s laugh at Christmas.

My current car also makes noises. Mostly not on purpose.

Round to Porsche, for the theatre. Nobody ever saved up for years to buy silence. Except parents. Parents would pay anything for silence.

Round 6: Price

A new 2027 Taycan starts at $111,900 in the US before delivery fees, and climbs fast. A new Model S starts at nothing at all, because you can no longer buy one new. Tesla has solved the affordability problem by removing the product.

My budget for either is €0, plus whatever kind strangers send me in imaginary espressos.

Round to Tesla, technically, for being the cheapest car I cannot buy.

Round 7: The family test

A saloon has to fit two children, a buggy, the beach bag, the other beach bag nobody admits to packing, and a wife who did not ask for any of this. Both cars have four doors and a boot. Both will be covered in biscuit crumbs within a week. Both will hear “are we there yet” at 2.2 seconds to 100.

The difference: in a Tesla my son would ask why the car has no buttons. In a Porsche he would ask why the electric car is going “vroom”. I prefer the second question. It has a better answer: “because Daddy is happy.”

Round to Porsche.

The verdict

Porsche wins, 4–2 with one draw. Partly on merit, and partly by walkover, like a football match where the other team turned up, looked at the pitch, and went off to build robots.

To be fair to Tesla, it did something no carmaker before it managed: it made an electric saloon that people wanted badly, and it pushed everyone else, Porsche included, to build a Taycan in the first place. Without the Model S there might be no Taycan for me to dream about. So thank you, Tesla. Enjoy the robots. If one of them folds laundry, call me.

As for me, the dream stays electric, stays Porsche, and stays scheduled for my birthday on 2 August 2027. If you think Tesla should have won, buy me an espresso anyway and I will consider a recount. If you think Porsche won, buy me an espresso to celebrate. Either way, if the fund falls short, or goes over €100,000, the money goes to the fight against cancer in Portugal. Even this rivalry ends well for somebody.

Not a road test. I have not driven these cars. This is a diary from a man raising money, one euro at a time, for a Taycan by 2 August 2027. Not affiliated with Porsche AG or Tesla, Inc.
